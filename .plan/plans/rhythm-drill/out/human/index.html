Brief

A Training drill where targets spawn on the beat of your own songs. They come as a mix of types, such as shaped flick targets and player figures that strafe in time with the music, and each vanishes if you don't hit it in time. A mix of Beat Saber and Guitar Hero in a synthwave world, built as an aim trainer.

The problem

Aim practice in Azimuth is effective but feels like work, and warming up is a chore. A rhythm drill gives the hands a steady, repeatable pace to settle into, set to music you chose, so warming up is something you want to start.

Who it is for

Azimuth players: Overwatch players from beginner to intermediate, on desktop with a mouse (mostly Chromium), who want to warm up to their own music.

Why now

v3 added a positional audio engine and a mature drill, level and coach framework, so a music-driven drill can reuse most of what exists.

Success looks like

You'd pick it to warm up. Add a song, pick a difficulty, targets land on the beat, and after a song or two your hand feels loose and accurate. It stands alone in Training and doesn't change the daily warm-up.

Constraints

  • stack TypeScript (strict; build runs tsc --noEmit) + Vite 8 + three.js 0.186. No UI framework: screens are template-string HTML in src/ui. fixed
  • stack Every drill extends Drill (src/drills/drill.ts), returns a DrillReport (0–100 score, stats, analytics) and is registered in DRILL_DEFS (src/drills/registry.ts) with 10 levels and 1/2/3★ thresholds. fixed
  • stack All sound goes through the single WebAudio Audio class (src/core/audio.ts), unlocked from a user gesture. Today it only synthesises sounds; it does not play samples or music files yet.
  • platform Static site on GitHub Pages: no server and no accounts. Uploaded audio never leaves the browser. fixed
  • platform Desktop Chromium is the main target (pointer lock with unadjustedMovement). Firefox must still work.
  • other Saves are small JSON in localStorage via store (src/core/store.ts), with backup export/import in Settings → Your data. Audio files cannot be stored there (~5 MB limit). fixed
  • performance Shots resolve at the instant of the click: Game.button() calls fire() and then drill.onShot() synchronously inside the mousedown handler (ShotResult.at is game-clock time).
  • other Tests are vitest in a node environment (tests/*.test.ts). Beat detection, chart building and scoring must be pure functions that run without WebAudio or the DOM.
  • legal No copyrighted music in the repo. There is no built-in song; test audio is generated by a test-only helper (D11). fixed
  • other All copy is in plain words for beginners, in the same voice as the coach and Field Manual.

Goals

G1 Drop in a song and be playing it within seconds, with no setup. must

Bringing your own music is the hook. Any friction before the first target kills it as a warm-up.

We will know it works when

  • Uploading an MP3, WAV or OGG file (plus M4A/AAC on Chromium) starts analysis immediately; an unsupported or broken file shows a plain-words error instead of failing silently.
  • A 4-minute song is analysed in under 5 s on a mid-range laptop, and the page never freezes for more than 100 ms while it runs.
  • Uploaded songs are stored in the browser (IndexedDB) and are still in the song list after a reload, with no re-upload.
  • The preset picker shown when adding a song has sensible defaults, so upload → playing takes at most one extra click.
  • The same song, preset and level always produce the same chart (seeded generator), so scores are comparable between runs.
  • Any song gets a chart with no hand-authoring: generation uses only the song's analysis, the preset and the level (D7).
G2 Targets land on the beat of the music. must

If timing feels off, it's not a rhythm drill, and an automatic chart has no fix-up step (D3).

We will know it works when

  • On generated test songs (drum loops at 70–190 BPM, with swing, noise and a quiet intro), detected tempo is within 1% and beat times within 20 ms of the truth (vitest, node).
  • During play, every target's spawn and hit time is computed from the AudioContext clock; drift between chart and audio stays under 10 ms across a 4-minute song.
  • Pausing stops music and targets together, and resuming continues in sync.
  • An audio offset (from a short tap-along calibration or set by hand) shifts every target; after calibration, the player's taps average within ±20 ms of the beat.
G3 Six target types each spawn on the beat, behave in time with the music and vanish if missed. must

A mix of target types is the core idea (D1, D2): each one trains a different aim skill inside one musical run.

We will know it works when

  • Flick shapes, strafers, holds, bursts, peekers and decoys all appear in charts.
  • Every target spawns on a beat (or eighth beat for bursts) and is removed when its hit window ends without a hit.
  • Strafers change direction on beats; holds move along their path in step with the beat; peekers show and hide on beats.
  • Hitting a decoy counts as a mistake and breaks the combo.
G4 It is a first-class Azimuth drill: levels, stars, debrief, Logbook and coaching. must

Players already understand Azimuth's progression. A rhythm drill outside it would feel like a toy.

We will know it works when

  • Registered in DRILL_DEFS with 10 levels and 1/2/3★ thresholds, and listed in Training like the other drills; not added to DAILY and the streak is unchanged (D15).
  • Picking a difficulty plays the highest unlocked level inside its band; a star on a level unlocks the next level in that band (D10, D17).
  • Its DrillReport gives a 0–100 score, the difficulty and level played, timing stats (mean early/late offset in ms) and hit rate per target type.
  • Flick-type hits produce FlickRecords, so the existing coach findings and film room work on them.
  • Runs appear in the Logbook's recent sessions.
G5 It feels like a rhythm game, not a test: flowing, musical and satisfying. should

The brief: a warm-up you want to start. Beat Saber and Guitar Hero are the references.

We will know it works when

  • Every hit gives instant feedback (sound, flash and a judgement word: Perfect, Early or Late) within one frame of the click; a missed target shows Miss as it vanishes.
  • A hit within the Perfect width of the target's beat scores more and adds more to the combo than a hit elsewhere in the window (D4).
  • A combo counter and multiplier build on consecutive hits and visibly break on a miss or a decoy hit.
  • The synthwave scene reacts to the music: the grid scrolls at the tempo and glows on the beat (D6).
  • Playtest: the human plays it on their own songs and says it feels on-beat and fun enough to warm up with.

Look and feel

Architecture

Decisions

D1 How do notes reach you? decided

Targets spawn in the 3D range on chosen beats, as a mix of target types that appear at different times. Each type behaves differently but in time with the music (e.g. a player figure that strafes to the beat, shaped flick icons that pop up rhythmically), and disappears if it isn't hit in time. The feel is a mix of Beat Saber and Guitar Hero, but as an aim trainer.

The human's own description, given in the terminal instead of picking one of the three single-mechanic concepts.

  • Highway Notes fly down a corridor to a frame around the crosshair.
  • Pop-in rings Notes appear in place with a shrinking ring.
  • Surround Notes arrive from all sides, Beat Saber 360° style.
  • Mixed target types on the beat Several target types spawn on beats, move to the music and vanish if missed.

Turned down: Highway only: every note flies down one corridor; Pop-in rings only: one kind of target with shrinking rings; Surround only: everything arrives from all sides

Affects: Overdrive

D2 Which target types are in the first version? decided

Flick shapes, Peekers, Holds, Decoys, Bursts, Strafers

Chosen by the human in "Which target types should be in the first version? Each one trains a different aim skill, and all of them spawn on the beat and vanish if missed."

  • Flick shapes Orbs and shaped icons pop up on the beat and must be shot before they fade. The shape tells you what kind of flick it is. Trains short and wide flicks.
  • Strafers A player figure spawns on a beat and strafes, changing direction on the beat (ADAD in time with the music). Take it down before the phrase ends or it leaves. Trains tracking.
  • Holds An orb glides along a path for a few beats. Hold fire and stay on it. Like Guitar Hero sustains and Beat Saber arcs. Trains smooth tracking.
  • Bursts A quick run of small orbs on fast beats (eighth notes) in a line or arc. Tap through them in order. Like Beat Saber chains. Trains quick switching.
  • Peekers A head peeks out from behind a wall on the beat and hides again on the next. Be there first. Trains crosshair placement.
  • Echo notes A ping from beside or behind you lands on the beat. Turn the short way and hit the target. Trains sound awareness, reusing the Echo drill's 3D audio.
  • Decoys A friendly figure is mixed into the pattern. Don't shoot it: hitting it breaks your combo. Trains trigger discipline.

D4 Do you have to hit on the beat, or just before the target is gone? decided

Window + beat bonus. A target appears on a beat and stays for a hit window. Any hit inside the window counts, but a hit close to the target's own beat scores Perfect and builds the combo faster. Higher levels shrink the window towards the beat.

Chosen by the human: rhythm feel without punishing the stop-then-click habit the coach teaches.

  • On the beat Exact beat, ±60 ms graded; else a miss.
  • Before it's gone Any hit in the window counts; sooner scores more.
  • Window + beat bonus Any hit in the window counts; on-beat hits score Perfect.

Consequences

  • Judgement words are Perfect (within the Perfect width of the target beat), Early (in the window, before that), Late (in the window, after it) and Miss (window ended).
  • Timing per target: launchTime = landTime − 1 beat (fly-in); landTime = targetTime − leadBeats; windowEnd = targetTime + afterBeats. leadBeats, afterBeats and the Perfect width come from the level table (T5).
  • Timing stats (mean signed offset in ms) come from hits, for the debrief and coach.

Turned down: On the beat only (strict ±60 ms); Before it's gone with no beat bonus

Combine all four cues: targets fly in from the distance, a ring closes onto each target on its beat, the target pulses with the music and flashes on its beat, and a Guitar Hero-style beat strip at the bottom of the screen shows upcoming targets.

The human's answer: "combine them".

  • Closing ring A ring shrinks onto the target, touching it on its beat.

  • Pulse Targets pulse with the music and flash on their beat.

Consequences

  • Each cue needs its own timing on the shared chart clock; see V3 for the proposed choreography.
  • Four cues at once risk clutter on dense charts; see R3.

D7 How are charts generated so that any song gets a good chart? decided

Each song is analysed for its structure (beats, bars, sections, energy, onsets). When you add a song you choose presets that shape how its chart is built: a style preset (a probabilistic matrix weighting target types and density per section kind) and a difficulty (D9). The generator samples spawns bar by bar from the style matrix, scaled by the level the difficulty resolves to, seeded by song + preset + level so the same inputs always give the same chart. No song needs a hand-made chart. The MVP ships one style preset, Mixed (D8); the others are on the roadmap.

The human's comments on V3 and D7: many songs in the long run, so charts must be generated from each song's own structure via a probabilistic decision matrix, with a few basic presets picked when adding a song to alter how it's structured. Start with one preset for the MVP so it can be tested, and keep the rest on the roadmap.

Consequences

  • Song analysis must find sections, not only beats.
  • Presets are data: one matrix per preset, plus optional multipliers on the level table's target parameters (e.g. strafer health × 1.5 for a future Tracker preset).
  • The seed uses the level, not the difficulty, because the level inside a band changes as you unlock (D17).
  • Adding a song shows a preset picker; with only Mixed in the MVP it defaults to Mixed so adding stays one step.
  • Roadmap presets: Quick taps, Tracker, Incoming (see scope, later).

D9 How does a song's difficulty relate to Azimuth's 1–10 drill levels? decided

A difficulty preset per song, Beat Saber style: Easy, Normal, Hard or Expert, picked when adding a song (and changeable before playing). Each maps to a band of drill levels (Easy 1–3, Normal 4–6, Hard 7–8, Expert 9–10), and within the band the chart follows the player's drill level.

Chosen by the human: it matches the Beat Saber/Guitar Hero feel and lets each song be a calm or intense run.

  • Levels are the difficulty No separate setting; play at an unlocked level.
  • Difficulty preset per song Easy/Normal/Hard/Expert mapped to level bands.
  • Free pick, stars tracked Any level for any song, nothing locked.

Consequences

  • Each song remembers its difficulty preset.
  • A rule is needed for picking a band above your unlocked level (D10).
  • The debrief and Logbook show the difficulty and the actual level played.

Turned down: Levels alone as the difficulty, with no per-song setting; Free pick of any level with no unlocks

D10 What happens if you pick a difficulty above your unlocked level? decided

All four difficulties are open from the start. The chart uses the band's lowest level, or your highest unlocked level if that falls inside the band. Stars count for the level actually played, and 1★ unlocks the next level as in every drill.

It keeps the Beat Saber freedom to pick Expert while staying on Azimuth's star progression, without a confusing lock screen.

  • Open, clamp into band Any difficulty; level clamped into the band.
  • Locked until unlocked Hard and Expert stay greyed out until you reach level 7 or 9.

D17 How do levels unlock inside a difficulty? decided

Each difficulty band unlocks on its own. You start a band at its lowest level, and a star on a level unlocks the next level in that band (Easy 1→2→3, Hard 7→8, and so on). The level played is the highest unlocked level in the chosen band. Other drills' progression is unchanged.

Chosen by the human. Azimuth's unlockedLevel() only counts consecutive stars from level 1, which would make stars on Hard or Expert unlock nothing.

  • Per-band unlock Stars unlock the next level within the band.
  • Global chain Keep Azimuth's rule: only consecutive stars from level 1 unlock.

Consequences

  • Overdrive needs its own helper (unlockedInBand) in src/rhythm/levels.ts; src/core/progress.ts stays as it is.

Turned down: Keep Azimuth's global unlock chain for Overdrive

Affects: D10 What happens if you pick a difficulty above your unlocked l…, G4 It is a first-class Azimuth drill: levels, stars, debrief,…

Risks

R1 Notes drift out of sync with the music. The game clock runs on requestAnimationFrame and freezes on pause, while music plays on the AudioContext clock, and output latency (especially Bluetooth headphones) adds 20–250 ms.

likelihood: high impact: high

Mitigation

Position every target from song time (AudioContext time minus output latency minus the audio offset), never from accumulated frame time. Pausing stops the music source and freezes song time; it does not suspend the shared AudioContext, so menu sounds keep working. A tap-along calibration and a manual slider set the audio offset.

R2 Automatic analysis picks the wrong tempo or beat phase on some songs (live recordings, swing, tempo changes, long quiet intros), so targets feel off-beat and there's no way for the player to fix it.

likelihood: medium impact: high

Mitigation

Use onset-strength autocorrelation with a tempo prior (prefer 80–180 BPM, resolving half and double tempo) and track beats per section rather than one global grid. Score the analysis confidence; when it's low, place targets on detected onsets (audible hits) instead of a strict grid. Test against generated songs with swing, tempo ramps and noise in vitest.

R3 Four cues at once (fly-in, ring, pulse, beat strip) clutter dense charts at high levels, so players can't see the targets they're aiming at.

likelihood: medium impact: medium

Mitigation

Keep each cue thin and low-contrast except at the moment it matters (ring and flash on the beat), cap targets on screen per level, and let the beat strip be switched off in Settings. Playtest level 10 on a fast song before tuning.

R4 Section detection mislabels the song (e.g. calls a quiet verse a peak, or finds no peak in a flat electronic loop), so charts feel random or monotonous.

likelihood: medium impact: medium

Mitigation

Classify sections by smoothed energy tiers and change points rather than trying to name verse or chorus; enforce a minimum section length (4 bars, snapped to bar boundaries); and fall back to energy-only density when the change points are weak. Unit-test on generated songs with known quiet/loud structure.

Glossary

Overdrive
The drill this plan builds (drill id 'overdrive').
Song
An audio file the player added to their library. There is no built-in song (D11). Avoid: track (means tracking aim in Azimuth), music file
Beat
One pulse of the song's tempo (a quarter note in 4/4). Targets spawn and must be hit relative to beats.
Bar
A group of beats, normally four. The generator works bar by bar. Avoid: measure
Section
A stretch of the song (at least 4 bars, endBar exclusive). Intro and outro are positional (the first and last sections only); middle sections are classified by energy as calm, build or peak. Avoid: part, verse/chorus
Chart
The timed list of targets (and walls) for one song at one preset and level. `Chart` in code. Avoid: beatmap, map
Style preset
Data that shapes a chart: target-type weights and targets per bar for each section kind, flick shape split, and optional multipliers on target parameters. `Preset` in code. MVP: Mixed only. Avoid: theme, mode
Difficulty
Easy, Normal, Hard or Expert, chosen per song. Maps to a band of drill levels (1–3, 4–6, 7–8, 9–10) that unlocks on its own (D17).
Level
The drill's 1–10 step, as in every Azimuth drill. The chart is built at one level inside the chosen difficulty's band.
Target
One thing to shoot that the chart spawns: a flick shape, strafer, hold, burst, peeker or decoy. `ChartTarget` in a chart. Avoid: note, block
Target type
A kind of target with its own look and behaviour (D2). `kind` on ChartTarget.
Target beat
The beat a target should be hit on. `targetTime` in code (seconds of song time).
Hit window
From landTime (the target lands) to windowEnd. Any hit inside counts; when it ends without a hit the target is missed and removed.
Judgement
How a hit is graded: Perfect (within the Perfect width of the target beat), Early or Late (elsewhere in the window), or Miss. Avoid: Good, Great
Song time
Seconds into the song as the player hears it: AudioContext time since the music started, minus output latency, minus the audio offset. Everything in a run is timed from it.
Beat cues
The four ways the beat is shown (D5): fly-in, closing ring, pulse and the beat strip.
Beat strip
The Guitar Hero-style HUD strip at the bottom of the screen where icons of upcoming targets scroll to a hit line. Avoid: highway, lane
Audio offset
How late the player hears the audio, in ms. It is subtracted in song time, so a positive offset makes targets arrive later. Set by the tap-along calibration or by hand.
